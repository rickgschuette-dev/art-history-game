# Vocabulary to revisit in later decks

Introduce these terms when a suitable artwork and a clear example make them useful to students. Add the term to Words to Know, use it in The Story Behind the Work when possible, and update source footnotes. Use pronunciation guides for unfamiliar foreign-language terms when helpful, following the user's choices for individual words.

- **Impasto:** Hold for Baroque or later, where thick paint is visible or specifically documented for the artwork.
- **Avant-garde:** Hold for the Modern deck and an appropriate historical example.
- **Kitsch:** Revisit with a suitable later work or discussion of popular imagery and taste.
- **Oeuvre:** Revisit when comparing several works by one artist provides a natural introduction.
- **Tenebrism:** Introduce with a suitable dark Caravaggio work in the Baroque deck, where figures are illuminated against a predominantly dark setting.
- **Coulisse:** Introduce in the Baroque deck when a work clearly uses side elements like stage wings to frame a scene and suggest depth; compare with repoussoir where helpful.
- **Chiaroscuro:** Introduce with Caravaggio in the Baroque deck; compare with tenebrism for his darkest works.
- **Trompe-l’œil:** Revisit with a clear example of an image designed to deceive the eye. Distinguish it from perspective or illusion of depth more generally.
- **Quadratura:** Revisit in a later deck with a ceiling or wall painting that appears to extend real architecture.
- **Di sotto in sù:** Revisit in a later deck with a ceiling painting designed to be seen from below.
- **Genre painting:** Revisit in a later deck with a clear scene of everyday life, especially in the Dutch Baroque.

The ongoing vocabulary review also includes pastiche, the gaze, liminality, and abjection; assess them one at a time before adding them.
